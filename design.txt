DOCTRINE - DESIGN DOCUMENT
==========================
Status: DRAFT 0.1 - captures the initial vision plus open questions.
Sections marked [OPEN] are undecided. Sections marked [PROPOSAL] are my
suggestions, not yet agreed.


1. VISION
---------
A web-based version of a tabletop war game set in a vaguely WW1 world.

- Hex map.
- Poker chips are units, defenses, terrain modifiers and so on.
- Playing cards are orders, and also define each nation's capabilities and
  doctrine.
- 2 players as the base case, more possible.
- No dice. Only ordinary poker chips and ordinary playing cards, so the game
  can be prototyped physically with household items.
- The web version is a DEVELOPMENT TOOL for the game design (fast iteration,
  playtesting, balancing), not the final product.

Core pillar: SIMPLICITY. Every rule must be explainable in a sentence and
resolvable without a calculator. A larger stack beats a smaller stack.

Second pillar: DOCTRINE. The cards you build in the early game decide what you
are good at and which orders you can issue later. Two nations should play
noticeably differently.


2. DESIGN PILLARS (used to settle arguments later)
--------------------------------------------------
1. Physical first. If it cannot be done with chips and a normal deck, it does
   not go in the rules (the web tool may add convenience, not rules).
2. Few numbers. Stack strength must be readable at a glance.
3. Doctrine is identity. Doctrine decides WHAT you can do, not just how big
   your numbers are.
4. Decisions over bookkeeping. Each turn offers real choices; arithmetic is
   minimal.
5. The war can be lost before the last chip is gone (morale / will to fight).


3. COMPONENTS
-------------
Physical equivalents (also the model for the web UI):

- Hex map board with terrain printed on it [OPEN: or terrain as chips/tiles?].
- Poker chips in one colour per player = that player's men.
- Additional chip colours/denominations for modifiers: gear, experience,
  defenses, terrain bonuses [OPEN: exact chip vocabulary, see section 8].
- One or more standard 52-card decks (+ jokers?) [OPEN: see section 9 - a
  standard deck has no text, so cards must be assigned meanings].
- Player mats: national capability area, doctrine area, reserve (unmobilized
  men).


4. GAME STRUCTURE - FOUR PHASES
-------------------------------
Buildup -> Mobilization -> War -> Peace

The buildup phase happens before the war starts. Mobilization and war follow.
Peace is triggered from inside the war phase.

4.1 BUILDUP PHASE (two parts)

  Part A - Nation
  - Each player draws an initial hand of cards describing the nation's core
    capabilities: population, economy, willingness to defend, aggressiveness,
    colonies, and anything else that is a core value of ability to wage war.
  - EXCLUDES military doctrine and terrain.
  - This is the basis for the rest of the game; it is given, mostly not chosen.

  Part B - Doctrine
  - Players draw and play cards to set their doctrine: propaganda,
    conscription/recruitment, weapons manufacturing and research, industrial
    improvements, large permanent defenses, plus the strategy of the armed
    forces (bonuses for attack, defense, support, multiple offensives, etc.).
  - Doctrine cards stay in play and govern which orders can later be played.

4.2 MOBILIZATION PHASE
  - Players draw and play cards; nation + doctrine determine the outcome.
  - Determines: how many men can be recruited, how fast they reach the front,
    initial equipment and experience bonuses, and what the standing
    professional army (present from the start) looks like.
  - Output: the starting chips on the map.

4.3 WAR PHASE
  - Chips move on the hex map. Players draw and play cards depending on their
    doctrine: offensives, building defenses, multiple simultaneous
    offensives, reorganizations, etc.
  - Combat: a larger stack defeats a smaller stack. Stacks are the sum of men,
    experience, gear, terrain and defenses. Some modifiers work only on
    defense (or only on offense).
  - Will to fight erodes. Causes: casualties, lost battles, too few victories,
    standstill, loss of territory.

4.4 PEACE PHASE
  - Triggered from the war phase by drawing a trigger card (e.g. a
    "surrender resolution" card).
  - Will to fight is calculated; one or more players may be forced to
    surrender and drop out. Game over for them, possibly for everyone.
  - Requirement: the trigger must NOT automatically end the war for every
    player. Only players who are actually broken should surrender.
    [OPEN: mechanism, see section 7]

6. COMBAT (draft)
-----------------
Principle: compare two stacks, the larger wins.

  Strength of a stack = men + experience + gear + terrain + defenses
  (offense-only / defense-only modifiers apply according to role)

- No dice. No randomness in the resolution itself. Randomness comes from card
  draws only (what orders and events you have).
- [OPEN: what does the loser lose? What does the winner lose? Ties? Retreat?]
- [OPEN: do surplus chips beyond the winning margin survive?]


7. WILL TO FIGHT AND SURRENDER (draft)
--------------------------------------
Each player has a will-to-fight track (starting value from nation cards).
Reduced by: casualties, lost battles, lost territory, standstill / no victories.
Increased by: victories, propaganda doctrine cards, etc.

Surrender is evaluated when a trigger card is drawn in the war phase.

[PROPOSAL] The trigger card is evaluated PER PLAYER: only players whose will
is at or below their threshold surrender; others carry on. That addresses
"make sure this isn't triggered for all".


8. CHIP VOCABULARY [OPEN]
-------------------------
Needs a decision. A list of candidates, in order of how much I would keep:

- Men chips (player colour). Base unit. 1 chip = 1 strength.
- Terrain modifier. Probably printed on the hex, not chips.
- Defense chips (trenches, forts). Defense only. Placed on a hex.
- Gear (artillery, machine guns, tanks, gas, ...).
- Experience.

Concern: four or five categories of modifier on every stack is where
simplicity usually dies. See section 11.


9. CARDS [OPEN]
---------------
Two ways to use "normal playing cards":

 a) Suits/ranks carry meaning (suit = category, rank = magnitude). Very
    physical, very abstract, a legend sheet is required.
 b) Printed custom cards. Easy online, harder to prototype physically (but
    paper cut-outs sleeved over normal cards work fine).

Card types identified so far:
- Nation cards (buildup A)
- Doctrine cards (buildup B)
- Mobilization cards
- War/order cards (offensive, build defenses, multi-offensive, reorganize,
  trigger/surrender, ...)

How doctrine gates orders: [OPEN] - see section 11.

-------------------------------

11. CRITICAL NOTES (my concerns so far)
---------------------------------------
Written to be argued with.

1. "No dice" removes the uncertainty that makes wars exciting. If a bigger
   stack always wins, the game is arithmetic and the best-resourced player
   wins. The only variance is cards. The fun has to come from hidden
   information (hand), timing, and bluffing about stack contents.
   -> Consider: gear/defense chips hidden face-down until a battle (a "fog"
   mechanic using stacked chips under a cover), or orders played face-down.

2. Four phases, each with its own card play, is a lot of rules before anything
   moves. Risk: the first 30+ minutes are drawing cards and building a
   spreadsheet. Buildup and mobilization both "convert cards into numbers" and
   could probably be a single phase.

3. Nation cards (capability) + doctrine cards + mobilization cards = three
   layers of cards that all say "you get more stuff". The distinction between
   them must be crisp or they will blur.

4. "Doctrine governs which cards can be played and when" is the interesting
   part, and also the hardest to keep simple. A rule that is short and
   physical: doctrine cards have a symbol/suit; an order card can be played
   only if you have a doctrine card with a matching symbol in front of you.
   One lookup, no arithmetic.

5. Stack strength = sum of five categories makes counting slow and error
   prone with physical chips. Prefer chips that already carry their strength
   (e.g. a gear chip is just "+1" and is the same colour/value).

6. Surrender: "any of 5 causes" is too many to track physically. Suggest a
   single will-to-fight chip pile per player: lose chips for casualties/lost
   battles/lost territory, gain them for victories. Surrender when it runs
   out. One resource, one check.

7. Surrender timing via a drawn card gives randomness, but might end the game
   at an anticlimactic or unfair moment. Needs a safeguard (e.g. cannot be
   triggered in the first X rounds, or is a countdown rather than instant).

8. More than 2 players multiplies everything: diplomacy, kingmaking,
   downtime, balance. I recommend designing and balancing strictly for 2
   players first, with 3-4 as an explicit later extension.

9. Hex map + stacking + movement + defense building + orders is already
   a full game; the card layer must not add a second game on top.


12. DECISIONS (from interview round 1)
--------------------------------------
D1. Uncertainty comes from ALL THREE sources: card draws, hidden information
    (face-down orders/modifiers revealed in battle), and simultaneous order
    selection. Caution: three sources is a lot for a 30-45 minute game. Plan
    to prototype with one primary source (suggest hidden/simultaneous orders)
    and treat the others as light seasoning. [OPEN: which is primary]

D2. Buildup and mobilization stay SEPARATE phases because they model
    different time scales:
      - Buildup = years of peacetime. Slow, structural. What can be ALTERED
        in doctrine happens here (doctrine is shaped, and mostly locked
        afterwards).
      - Mobilization = weeks. Fast execution. Doctrine + nation determine
        the outcome; few decisions of their own.
    Consequence: mobilization should be short and mostly "resolve what your
    doctrine says", while buildup is where the real choices are. [PROPOSAL]
    Doctrine can only be changed in buildup; later "reorganization" orders in
    the war phase can only swap among doctrine options already held.

D3. Cards: standard deck for the physical prototype; custom printed cards in
    the web version. Rule: any web card must be expressible as suit + rank
    (+ a legend sheet) so the physical version stays possible.

D4. Doctrine gating by SYMBOL MATCHING: doctrine cards carry a symbol/suit; an
    order card can be played only if a matching doctrine card is in play.
    Natural fit with standard deck suits (4 suits = 4 doctrine families,
    e.g. clubs = attrition, spades = defense, hearts = manpower/morale,
    diamonds = industry/gear - names are placeholders).

D5. Will to fight is HIDDEN and calculated only when the surrender card is
    drawn. To keep this physical and cheap, it must be derived from visible
    piles, not tracked live:
      - casualty pile (men removed from the map go there; its size = losses)
      - battles won/lost tokens
      - territory held (count hexes / capital status)
    The tension comes from not knowing exactly where the threshold is.
    [OPEN: threshold per nation comes from nation cards, kept face-down?]

D6. 2 players only for now. Multiplayer is a later extension.

D7. Target play length: 30-45 minutes. This is tight with four phases:
    suggested budget -> buildup <= 8 min, mobilization <= 4 min, war
    25-30 min, peace 2 min.


13. DECISIONS (from interview round 2)
--------------------------------------
D8. PRIMARY uncertainty = card draws (what orders you hold). Hidden/
    simultaneous orders are dropped as core mechanics (may return as optional
    variants). Alternating turns, so no simultaneous resolution is needed.

D9. Chip vocabulary: three categories only.
      - Men (player colour)
      - Gear / experience (veteran-quality modifiers, one category)
      - Defense: natural (terrain, printed on hex) and man-made (trenches,
        forts - chips). Defense modifiers apply only to the defender.

D10. Combat: the larger stack wins. The loser retreats; BOTH sides lose 1 chip.
     Fast, low attrition.

D11. War phase turns alternate; each player plays up to N order cards per turn
     [OPEN: N, hand size].

D12. Win condition: surrender only, driven by will to fight. No separate
     capital/victory-point condition.

D13. Surrender safeguard: each drawn surrender card checks ONLY the player who
     drew it.


14. TENSIONS CREATED BY THE DECISIONS (to resolve)
--------------------------------------------------
T1. Low attrition (D10) vs casualty-based will to fight (D5). If each battle
    costs both sides 1 chip, the casualty pile grows slowly and equally for
    both players, so casualties are a weak signal. Will to fight will be
    driven mainly by territory lost, battles lost, and standstill. Either
    drop casualties as a main input or scale them (e.g. chips lost by the
    loser count double).

T2. Surrender-only win (D12) + low attrition + alternating card turns = risk
    of a long grind where nobody breaks. Needs a fallback: the war deck is
    finite and the surrender card is guaranteed to appear (e.g. last card);
    if the deck runs out, both players are checked.

T3. The drawer-only check (D13) is lopsided: a winning player who draws it
    suffers nothing, a losing player who draws it may surrender. This makes
    the loser's draws dangerous ("hot potato"), which may be interesting but
    needs playtesting. Possible tweak: a drawn surrender card is kept in hand
    and can be played on either player (agency for the one who is ahead).

T4. Card draws as primary uncertainty with orders gated by doctrine symbols
    (D4): a player may hold orders they cannot play. Known source of
    frustration. Mitigation: discard/redraw one card per turn.

16. DECISIONS (from interview round 3)
--------------------------------------
D14. Cards per turn and hand size are NOT fixed: doctrine can raise or lower
     the number of cards played, permanently or temporarily. [OPEN: base
     values. Proposal for the baseline: 2 cards per turn, hand of 5.]

D15. Combat result (refines D10): the LOSER chooses how to pay:
       a) Stand and hold: lose 2-3 men, no retreat.
       b) Retreat: lose 1 man plus 1-2 gear/experience.
     The attacker loses 1 man and gains experience/gear.
     [OPEN: does "gains" apply to the winner of any battle, or only to a
     winning attacker? Where does gained gear/exp come from (supply pool)?
     Suggested: the winner, attacker or defender, gains 1 exp.]
     This also resolves T1: casualties are now larger and a real choice.

D16. Surrender cards are placed in SEVERAL places in the deck; no hard end
     (rejects T2 proposal). Checks only the drawer (D13 confirmed, test as
     is). Risk acknowledged: a grind is possible; revisit after playtests.

D17. Unplayable cards (T4): doctrine-less GENERAL ORDERS exist that any
     player can always play (e.g. move, hold, basic attack).

D18. Order suits (tentative): aggressive action, defensive action,
     reorganizational action, will-to-fight action.
     NOTE: D4 says doctrine cards carry the symbol and gate orders, so these
     four suits double as doctrine families: you can play an order of a suit
     only if you hold a doctrine card of that suit. [OPEN: confirm.]
     The 4th suit (willingness to fight) is the one that touches the
     surrender mechanic (propaganda, morale, rallies) and would counter
     surrender checks.

D19. Map size: large enough to feel like a front: up to about ten stacks per
     side. [OPEN: hex dimensions; proposal ~ 12 x 8 hexes.]

18. MINIMAL PROTOTYPE SCOPE (v0) [PROPOSAL]
-------------------------------------------
Purpose: answer three questions as cheaply as possible.
  Q1. Does doctrine symbol-gating make the two sides play differently?
  Q2. Is the loser's "hold or retreat" choice interesting?
  Q3. Does the hidden, drawer-only surrender check feel tense or random?
Everything else is deferred.

18.1 IN SCOPE
  Setup (skips buildup and mobilization entirely):
    - 2 players, hot-seat (one screen; during testing there is no hand cover,
      End turn can be pressed repeatedly to pass).
    - Map: the Western Front, Paris to the Ruhr, 25 x 26 hexes of ~20 km
      (data/map.json, generated by tools/genmap.mjs from real lon/lat) with
      five countries (France, Germany, Belgium, Netherlands, Luxembourg) drawn
      with their 1914 borders, and rivers as blue lines. Terrain modifiers, each
      worth chips of defence for the defender, and they STACK: forest, hills,
      marsh (+1 each), mountains (+2), river (+1), big river (+2), city (+1),
      big city (+2), fortress (+2). A hex is e.g. "hills+bigriver+city" = +4.
      Rivers mark every hex they run through (Rhine, Meuse, Moselle, Seine,
      Marne, Aisne, Oise, Somme, Scheldt, Lys, Yser, Sambre, Ruhr...); the
      lower Rhine, lower Meuse and Scheldt estuary are big. Sea is impassable.
      Legacy "rough" (+1) and "fortified" (+2) still work. Capitals: Paris (A)
      and Cologne (B). The north edge is the Dutch coast at Rotterdam (Amsterdam
      is off the map); the south edge reaches Epinal and Colmar.
    - Stacks per side pre-placed as the 1914 armies (see M4b), 3-5 men chips
      each, 1 chip = 30,000 men. Belgium, Luxembourg and the Netherlands start empty.
    - Doctrine (SUPERSEDED by M5, see 18.6): a preset of 6 doctrine cards
      whose per-suit count sets the magnitude of that suit's orders:
        Player A: 3 Aggressive, 2 Reorganizational, 1 Defensive
        Player B: 3 Defensive, 2 Willingness, 1 Reorganizational
    - Each player draws a face-down surrender threshold (8-12, plus 2 per
      Willingness card). Hidden, from "nation" standing in for the later
      nation cards (D5).

  Chips: men, gear/exp (single +1 "veteran" chip), man-made defense (+1,
  defender only, max 2 per hex).

  Cards - one 40-card war deck, shared draw pile; hand of 5, play up to 2
  cards per turn, draw back to 5 at end of turn:
    General orders (no doctrine needed): Move, Hold, Basic Attack.
    Aggressive: Offensive (a pool of points, see M6; replaces Double
      Offensive).
    Defensive: Entrench (place a defense chip), Counterattack (+1 when
      defending, reaction-free: played on own turn only).
    Reorganizational: Merge/Split stacks, Rally (recover 1 gear/exp).
    Willingness: Propaganda (-3 on your break score), Martial Law (-2 on
      break score, but lose 1 man).
    Surrender: 3 copies shuffled through the deck. Drawn card checks only the
      drawer (D13). Not playable; resolved immediately when drawn.

  Rules:
    - Each stack acts at most once per turn (move 1 hex or attack 1 adjacent
      hex).
    - Strength = men + gear/exp (+ terrain + defense chips if defending).
    - COMBAT (revised in M7 and M8, see 18.6): attacker and defender both
      lose men (attacker: the defender's terrain + trench chips, defender:
      1 + Offensive points).
      Larger strength has the upper hand; a tie is a stalemate. If the
      attacker has the upper hand the defender chooses STAND (1 more man)
      or RETREAT (1 gear, else 1 man). Otherwise nothing more happens.
    - Lost men go to the owner's casualty pile.
    - Break score = casualty pile + 2 per battle lost + 1 per hex the player
      started with that the enemy now holds, minus Willingness card effects.
      A player surrenders when break score >= hidden threshold, checked
      only when a surrender card is drawn by them.
    - Game ends on surrender. If the draw pile runs out, reshuffle the
      discards (no hard end, per D16).

18.2 WEB IMPLEMENTATION
  - Static site (GitHub Pages), vanilla HTML/CSS/JS with ES modules, no build
    step. SVG hex map.
  - Rules engine = pure functions (state in, state out) in its own module, so
    it can be unit-tested with Node and later reused for a networked version.
  - Cards, map and doctrine definitions in JSON, editable without code.
  - UI enforces legal moves and highlights them (no rules lawyering).
  - Game log of every action, exportable as JSON, plus a "new game" button

18.3 OUT OF SCOPE FOR v0
  Nation cards, buildup phase, mobilization phase, peace phase beyond a

18.4 MILESTONES
  M1. Hex map, terrain, chips and stacks rendered; select and move stacks.
  M2. Combat resolution with the hold/retreat choice and gear/exp gain.
  M3. War deck, hands, doctrine gating, playing orders.
  M4. Surrender cards, hidden thresholds, game end, hot-seat hand cover.
  M5. Game log/export, seed, rules-engine tests.
  Each milestone is playable on its own.

18.5 SUCCESS CRITERIA (after ~5 playtests)
  - A game finishes in 30-45 minutes.
  - Both fixed doctrines feel different and neither always wins.
  - Players report real decisions in combat and in which cards to play.
  - The surrender draw is surprising but not felt as pure luck.
  If these fail, the doc is revised before adding more rules.


18.6 IMPLEMENTATION STATUS AND RULE DETAILS DECIDED IN CODE
-----------------------------------------------------------
M1 (map, chips, movement), M2 (combat) and M3 (deck, hands, doctrine gating,
orders) are implemented. Details that the rules above left open and that were
fixed in code (change them here, in js/engine.js and data/cards.json if
playtests disagree):
  - A stack needs at least 2 men to attack (the attack itself costs 1 man).
  - The attacker always loses 1 man; the winner of the battle gains 1 gear.
  - Ties go to the defender. Terrain bonus: the sum of every modifier on the
    hex (see 18.1), defender only. Trench chips (Entrench, max 2 per hex) add +1 each and stay on the
    hex whoever holds it. A braced stack (Hold / Defensive Posture) gets +1
    until its owner's next turn; moving, attacking or retreating removes it.
  - The loser chooses: HOLD (lose 2 men, stay) or RETREAT (lose 1 man and
    1 gear if any, move to an empty adjacent hex that is not adjacent to the
    winner). A stack with fewer than 2 men cannot retreat and must hold.
  - A winning attacker advances into the defender's hex if the defender
    retreated or was destroyed. A winning defender never advances.
  - Stacks reduced to 0 men are removed. Men lost go to the owner's casualty
    pile; lost battles are counted per player (both feed the break score in
    M4).
  - Observation: because the attacker pays 1 man up front, a failed attack
    by a 3-man stack costs 3 men (hold) or 1 man + 1 gear + 1 man (retreat).
    Attacking is expensive; check in playtests that it is not too
    expensive.

  M3 decisions:
  - EVERY action costs a card play: the free per-stack move/attack of M1/M2
    is gone. A stack may be used by several cards in one turn (limit is the
    2 plays per turn, doctrine can change this later per D14).
  - Playing a card "arms" an order; then the player selects the stack(s) and
    targets. An unused armed order can be cancelled (card and play are
    refunded); a partly used one (Double Offensive) can be finished early.
  - A card cannot be played if no own stack could use it ("No stack can use
    this order") - e.g. attack cards with no enemy adjacent are greyed out.
  - Doctrine: SUPERSEDED by M5 below (no gating; suit counts set magnitude).
  - One shared 37-card deck (the 3 surrender cards arrive with M4 to make 40):
    general 16 (Move 8, Basic Attack 6, Hold 2), aggressive 6 (Offensive 4,
    Double Offensive 2), defensive 6 (Entrench 4, Defensive Posture 2),
    reorganizational 6 (Merge 2, Split 2, Rally 2), willingness 3
    (Propaganda 2, Martial Law 1).
  - Hands refill to 5 at the end of your own turn. When the deck is empty
    the discard pile is reshuffled (seeded RNG, reproducible).
  - ADDED in v0 (not in the earlier rules): one free discard per turn, so a
    hand clogged with off-doctrine cards (a shared deck guarantees these)
    cannot deadlock a player. Redrawn at end of turn.
  - Offensive = +1 strength on that attack. Double Offensive = two different
    stacks may each attack once. Hold = one stack +1 defense. Defensive
    Posture = all stacks +1 defense. Entrench = +1 trench chip on a stack's
    hex. Merge = a stack joins an adjacent friendly stack (men and gear
    add). Split = a stack of 2+ men sends floor(men/2) men (no gear) to an
    adjacent empty hex. Rally = a stack with < 2 gear gains 1 gear.
  - Propaganda = resolve +3 and Martial Law = resolve +3 plus a stack
    (2+ men) loses 1 man (counted as a casualty, so net -2 on the break
    score, matching the earlier "-2 with a man lost"). `resolve` is stored
    per player and is consumed by the break score (M4).
  - Only the current player's hand is shown (the opponent's is a count).

  M4 decisions (surrender and game end):
  - 3 Surrender cards are added to the deck after the opening hands are
    dealt (deck 40 at start of war), so nobody is checked before play begins.
    A drawn Surrender card is never kept: it is resolved at once and goes to
    the discard pile (it comes back after a reshuffle). The drawer then
    draws a replacement card.
  - Only the DRAWER is checked (D13): they surrender if
    break score >= their hidden threshold. If not, the log says "the nation
    holds firm" - which tells the opponent the threshold is above the score.
  - Break score = casualties + 2 per battle lost + 1 per enemy stack standing
    in the player's territory (3 if on their capital) - resolve (never below
    0). Territory = the player's half of the map (see M4b). "Held" = an
    enemy stack is standing on it (no zones of control). All weights are in
    data/cards.json under "surrender".
  - Thresholds: each player gets a random 8-12 from the seeded RNG, stored
    in the game state and not shown until the game ends. The break scores
    themselves are shown to both players (they are countable from public
    chips anyway: casualty pile, lost battles, lost ground).
  - Also ends the game: a player with no stacks left surrenders at once.
  - The end screen shows the winner, why, and both break scores and
    thresholds, so playtesters can see how close it was.
  - Hot-seat cover: ending a turn hides the hand behind "Pass the device to
    Player X" until they press "Show my hand". End turn is disabled while the
    hand is covered. The board is always visible.
  - Observation for playtests: 3 of 40 cards are Surrender and a turn draws
    up to 3 cards (2 plays + the free discard), so one comes up roughly every
    4-5 turns, alternating between the players at random. If games drag, add
    copies or lower thresholds; if a break feels like pure luck, narrow the
    threshold range.

  M4b decisions (setting and layout):
  - Territory follows the countries: France is Player A's (red tint), Germany
    Player B's (blue tint); Belgium, Luxembourg and the Netherlands are neutral
    ground (own tints) and count for nobody. Borders are pale lines drawn along
    hex edges (every hex belongs to exactly one country); rivers are lines
    through hex centres, and every hex on one carries the river modifier.
    Set by "countries" (one letter per hex) and "owners" in data/map.json;
    "borderCol" is the straight-line default for maps without countries.
    Territory does not change hands
    when occupied; an enemy stack standing in it simply counts for the break
    score. (Idea for later: occupation flips ownership, ties into peace.)
  - Armies start as in August 1914, from educated guesses (not exact orders of
    battle): France's 1st-5th Armies in Lorraine and the Ardennes edge (34 chips
    ~ 1.0 million men), Germany's 1st-7th Armies from Aachen down to Alsace-
    Lorraine (48 chips ~ 1.4 million). The BEF, the Belgian army and the French
    Army of Alsace are left out. One men chip = 30,000 soldiers (about a corps,
    `menPerChip` in data/map.json). Each army is split into stacks of at most 5
    chips (3-5 here), set by ARMIES in tools/genmap.mjs; stacks carry an `army`
    name shown on selection. Every stack stands in its own country and the two
    sides are at least 2 hexes apart, so nobody can attack on turn 1. No stacks
    start in the neutral countries, which stay open for flank marches.
    Capitals are at the back (Cologne is now close to the front: Aachen is ~3 hexes away).
  - Stack size varies with the army (was a flat 5, originally 3). Strength is
    5 at most vs the 8-12 break thresholds: watch in playtests whether the
    thresholds should rise with the bigger armies.
  - Gear/experience is a physical chip: each gear point adds a gold chip on
    top of the stack, the same colour for both players (label "men+gear").

  M5 decisions - DOCTRINE AS MAGNITUDE (supersedes the M3 doctrine gating):
  - Idea (from the designer): the doctrine is a SET OF CARDS; the number of
    cards of each suit decides how strongly that suit's orders work. Nothing
    is gated any more: every player can play every card.
  - Doctrine = 6 cards (budget), dealt face up as a preset (data/cards.json
    "doctrine"). Each suit's level = number of doctrine cards of that suit,
    capped at 3. Level 0 is allowed and means a weak version of the order.
    The budget forces a trade-off: more of one suit means less of another.
    Physically: lay the 6 cards in front of you, grouped by suit.
  - Level tables (data/cards.json "levels"), index = level 0,1,2,3:
      Aggressive  Offensive points     0  1  2  3   (see M6)
      Defensive   Entrench max/hex     1  2  3  3
                  Defensive Posture    +1 +1 +2 +3 defense
      Reorg.      Rally while gear <   1  2  3  3
                  Split takes gear     0  0  0  1
      Willingness Propaganda resolve   2  3  4  5
                  Martial Law resolve  3  4  5  6
    Level 1 equals the old fixed behaviour (offensive +1, trench max 2,
    rally limit 2, propaganda 3, martial 3). General cards (Move, Basic
    Attack, Hold) are unscaled.
  - Willingness also raises the hidden surrender threshold by 2 per
    Willingness doctrine card (uncapped count): a willing nation breaks
    later. Player B (2 Willingness) draws 12-16 against A's 8-12.
  - Visibility: a player may DECLARE the doctrine (cards face up, public)
    for +2 starting resolve, or keep it SECRET (hidden from the opponent,
    no bonus). Choice per player via checkboxes in the header, applied on
    New game. Both doctrines are revealed on the end screen. (Interpretation
    of "face up is stronger in itself, but the choice is up to the player".)
  - Card text and the hand show the owner's level (e.g. "aggressive - L3")
    and the real numbers, so the player sees what a card does for them.
  - Not done yet: drafting the doctrine in the buildup phase (preset only),
    a Reform card to move a doctrine card between suits mid-war.
  - Balance watch: A (attack 3) vs B (defence 3, +4 threshold). B looks
    stronger in defence and tougher to break; check in playtests.

  M6 decisions - OFFENSIVE POINTS AND CHIP TERRAIN:
  - Problem: at level 3 Aggressive had no real choice (just a bigger number).
    Fix: the Offensive card (6 copies, Double Offensive removed) gives a POOL
    of points equal to the Aggressive level (0,1,2,3). The first attack is
    free. Each point adds +1 strength to one attack, OR 2 points buy one
    extra attack by a different stack. Bonuses are chosen per attack (+0..+max
    buttons in the order panel) before clicking the target.
      Level 3 examples: one attack at +3; two attacks, one of them +1;
      Level 2: one attack +2, or two attacks at +0. Level 1: one attack +1.
  - Why it balances: spreading is expensive (2 points, and every attack
    already costs the attacker a man), concentrating helps one battle only
    and the defender still wins ties. Tuning knobs, all in data/cards.json:
    "extraCost" on Offensive and the "points" row in the level table. No cap
    on the bonus for now; add one (e.g. max +2) if +3 on one stack proves
    too decisive.
  - Terrain is now shown as chips, all the same size, in ONE pile per hex,
    bottom to top: terrain chips (one per point of every modifier, coloured
    by modifier: forest green, river blue, city grey...), trench
    chips (dark), men (owner colour), gear (gold). Hexes themselves are all
    one colour. Terrain and trench chips are +1 defense each, defender only,
    never move or attack. Rules and map data are unchanged (only the
    rendering moved to chips), so a physical board can build the same piles
    on a plain hex mat. An empty hex with terrain shows just its chips.

  M7 decisions - ATTRITION COMBAT (supersedes the combat bullets in the
  first M2 notes above):
  - Strength is compared first (attacker: men + gear + Offensive bonus;
    defender: men + gear + terrain + trench chips + brace). Then BOTH sides
    always lose 1 man (attrition, counted as casualties, so every fight
    feeds both break scores).
  - Attacker larger: upper hand. It gains 1 gear and the defender's player
    gets a lost battle. The surviving defender chooses:
      STAND  - lose 1 more man (2 in total with attrition), keep the hex.
      RETREAT - lose 1 gear, or 1 man if it has no gear, and move to an
               empty adjacent hex not adjacent to the attacker. Needs a
               chip to spare (gear, or 2+ men) and a free hex; otherwise
               it must stand. The attacker then advances into the hex.
    If attrition destroys the defender, no choice is needed and the
    attacker advances.
  - Defender larger: the attack is repulsed. The attacker pays only the
    attrition man, the defender gains 1 gear, the attacker's player gets a
    lost battle. Nobody chooses.
  - Tie: stalemate. Attrition only, nobody moves, no gear, no lost battle.
    (Replaces "defender wins ties".) The attacker is never forced to choose;
    the retreat/stand choice is always the defender's.
  - Why: attacks now cost the defender too, so attacking bleeds the enemy's
    will to fight (Aggressive gains purpose beyond winning fights), while
    terrain, trenches and bracing decide who gets the upper hand.
  - Watch in playtests: a failed attack costs the same as a tie (1 man
    each), so attacking at bad odds is cheap; consider making a repulsed
    attacker lose 1 extra man. Casualties accrue faster, so the 8-12
    surrender thresholds probably need to rise.
    (Both addressed in M8.)

  M8 decisions - THE WESTERN FRONT FEEL (supersedes the flat 1-man
  attrition of M7; the stand/retreat/repulse/tie structure stays):
  Research basis (Wikipedia: Somme, Verdun, Passchendaele, Defence in depth):
    * Somme: 57,470 British casualties on day one (19,240 dead), over a
      million casualties all sides, for a few km. Attackers bled as much
      as defenders.
    * Verdun: designed to bleed France of men and will, not to take ground.
    * Defence in depth: yield space, let the attack lose momentum, counter-
      attack. Prepared defences made attackers pay. (From general
      knowledge, not checked: ~3:1 local superiority rule of thumb; French
      mutinies after Nivelle 1917; wars ended when will broke.)
  Rules:
  1. BLOODLETTING (replaces the flat 1 man each).
       Attacker loses the defender's terrain chips + trench chips (min 1).
       Defender loses 1 + the Offensive points spent on the attack.
     Aggressive doctrine makes the enemy bleed; Defensive doctrine and
     terrain make the attacker bleed. Whoever has the upper hand, both pay.
       Somme example: 6 men + 3 pts vs 5 men on fortified + 2 trenches is
       9 vs 9: stalemate, the attacker loses 4, the defender loses 4.
  2. SPENT ATTACKERS: REMOVED. A stack that advanced used to be spent (-1
     strength until its owner's next turn). The counter-attack window it gave
     is now left to bloodletting (the attacker pays for terrain and trenches),
     the free enemy-ZOC rule (an advance can be hit by stacks next to it),
     war exhaustion and surrounded stacks. The bot simulation barely moved
     (about 10-12 wins for A of 40 either way).
  3. WAR WEARINESS (the standstill clock): at the end of each of a nation's
     turns in which it captured no hex, its weariness rises by 1 and counts
     in its break score. Capturing a hex resets it to 0. In a stalemate
     both nations tire; willingness doctrine and Propaganda decide who
     breaks first. Gains justify the losses; no gains, no tolerance.
  4. DEPTH: map.json now holds "defense": 1 trench chip on each starting
     front-line hex, a second line (3 hexes per side, 1 chip), and 2 trench
     chips on each capital. Surrender thresholds rise from 8-12 to 14-20
     (+2 per willingness card as before).
  5. CAPITAL SIEGE (victory by gains): if a nation has a stack on the enemy
     capital at the end of its own turn and that stack is still there at
     the end of the enemy's next turn, the war is won outright. Breaking
     will remains the other way to win; it is checked only on surrender
     cards as before.
  Simulation (tools/simulate.mjs, heuristic bots, 300 seeded games):
    * Mirror doctrine: 49/51 split A/B. No first-mover advantage.
    * Default doctrines (A aggressive 3 vs B defensive 3): ~90% A. About
      13 rounds, ~8 A / ~14 B casualties of 25 men; 70% end on surrender
      cards, 28% on capital sieges, rarely total destruction.
    * Doctrines closer together (B aggressive 2, defensive 2) are ~50/50.
  Open: a pure defensive doctrine loses to the naive bot. The bot never
  counter-attacks cleverly, so this needs human playtests. If it holds up:
  min attacker loss 2 (tested: 82/18 and casualties 11.5/14.9 instead of
  8.2/14.5), a defensive weariness relief, or stronger Defensive levels.
  Not built: manpower pools and replacements, wrong-way trenches.

M9 - ZONES OF CONTROL, FREE MOVEMENT AND DOCTRINE POINT MENUS
  Supersedes: all Move cards, and the separate Entrench, Merge, Split, Rally,
  Propaganda and Martial Law cards listed above. Their effects now live in the
  pool cards below. Decisions confirmed by the designer.
  1. ZONE OF CONTROL: every stack with at least 1 man controls the 6 adjacent
     hexes. Hexes controlled by both sides are contested.
  2. FREE MOVEMENT: no card needed. Each stack may step 1 hex per turn into an
     empty hex that is NOT in an enemy zone. Leaving a zone is allowed. A stack
     that fought this turn cannot step.
  3. ASSAULT: entering an enemy zone needs an attack order. The stack uses its
     step to move into an empty hex touching an enemy it is not yet adjacent
     to, then attacks from there. Entry costs 2 Offensive points, otherwise the
     assault is HASTY: -2 strength. Stacks already in contact attack for free.
     Counter-attack (defensive card) enters free but only hits a stack that
     just advanced (spent).
  4. START: armies stand one hex apart (distance 2) on a zigzag front; the front
     is sealed, so no unit can slip past the lines.
  5. RETREAT: only into empty hexes outside every enemy zone. Elastic Defence
     (defensive, immediate) makes retreats free until the owner's next turn.
  6. POINT MENUS: each doctrine suit has a pool card whose level gives points.
     Points are spent on options of different cost, so one card is a choice:
       Aggressive (Offensive)  +1 strength | 2 = extra attack | 2 = clean entry
       Defensive (Fortify)     1 = trench chip on a stack's hex (max per hex
                               by level); Posture, Counter-attack, Elastic
       Reorganizational        Logistics: 1 rail (+1 hex), 2 rally (+1 gear,
                               up to a level cap), 1 merge, 1 split
       Willingness (Home Front) 1 propaganda (+1 resolve), 1 relief (-2
                               weariness), 2 recruit (+1 man in own territory)
     Pool cards are only a budget; the player chooses where it goes.
  7. STEPPING OUT OF A ZONE: a stack already in an enemy zone may attack in
     place (an enemy is always adjacent) or step free into a hex outside every
     enemy zone. It cannot step from one enemy zone into another.
  8. SUPPLY: a stack needs a path to its own capital that crosses no enemy zone
     and no enemy stack (its own hex and the capital hex are exempt). Without
     one it is CUT OFF: it cannot act, merge, split or retreat (it must stand),
     loses 1 gear on its first cut-off turn and 1 man on each turn after that
     (checked at the start of its owner's turn; counts as casualties). It
     recovers the moment a path reopens. Open question: "a stack of men" was
     read as one man per turn.
  Simulation (300 seeded games, naive bots): default doctrines A 64% / B 36%,
  ~18 rounds, casualties ~12 / ~16, mirrored doctrines 50/50. Bots mostly take
  hasty assaults and never use rail or split, so this needs human playtests.
  Not built: manpower pools, doctrine drafting, nation cards, game-log export.

M10 - PLAYING CARDS, TIERS, LONGER WALKS (supersedes M3 deck, M9 items 2, 3, 7,
     the "attacker gains 1 gear / defender gains 1 gear" lines of M7, and
     the spent-stack idea of M8 item 2 and M9 item 3)
  Decisions confirmed by the designer; numbers are playtest guesses.
  1. DECKS: one standard 52-card deck per player, no jokers. Ranks 2-10, J, Q,
     K, Ace (high). A spent deck is refilled from that player's own discards.
     Hand 7 (was 5, see item 17), two plays per turn, one free discard per turn.
  2. SUITS (fixed function): spades = Offensive (the only way to attack),
     clubs = Defence, diamonds = Logistics, hearts = Home Front. Each suit is
     tied to one doctrine suit (aggressive, defensive, reorganizational,
     willingness), so a nation's doctrine level in that suit shifts the card.
  3. TIERS: a rank has a natural tier: 2-4 = 0, 5-7 = 1, 8-10 = 2, J-K = 3,
     Ace = 4. Doctrine shifts the tier by level 0/1/2/3 = -2/-1/0/+1, so a
     level 3 doctrine lifts an 8-10 to the tier of a face card and a face card
     above it. Tiers never go below 0. Per-tier tables in data/cards.json give
     the points (and trench/rally limit). A split halves both men and gear:
     the new stack takes the lower half (men and gear), the old keeps the rest.
  4. SETS (formerly pairs): any number of cards of one suit may be played
     together as one order; faces and Aces may join but lose their abilities
     (they count as their tier). Each card's points (tier table, doctrine
     shift per card) add up, plus 1 for each card beyond the first; the
     trench/rally limit comes from the strongest card. A 2 and a 3 (2 + 2 + 1
     = 5 spade points at tier +1) beat either card alone. The set uses all its
     cards but counts as one order (see items 18 and 20).
  5. FACE CARDS (J, Q, K, A) add an ability on top of their tier:
       Spades    J Surprise (defender's trench chips do not count)
                 Q Combined arms (extra attacks cost 1 point)
                 K No quarter (a beaten defender cannot retreat)
                 A Breakthrough (loser -1 man more)
       Clubs     J Elastic defence   Q Reserves (free trench chip on every
                 stack facing the enemy)
                 K Iron wall (+1 point, trench limit +1)   A all of these
       Diamonds  J Rail net (+3 hexes)   Q Staff work (+2 points)
                 K Depots (rally costs 1, limit +1)        A all of these
       Hearts    J War bonds (relief 2)            Q Relief effort (relief 3)
                 K Conscription (recruits cost 1)  A relief 3 + cheap recruits
  6. ATTACKING: only a spade order lets a stack attack. One stack attacks an
     ADJACENT enemy for free (no assault entry, no hasty penalty); points are
     +1 strength each, or extraCost (2) for another attack by another stack.
     Doctrine governs the points, so a nation with no aggressive doctrine
     gets a bare attack from every spade.
  7. WALKING: every stack walks 2 hexes per turn without a card (rail adds
     more). Entering an enemy zone of control is free but ends the walk, and
     so does entering a hex with terrain or trench chips (rail ignores that
     second stop); a stack that starts inside a zone can only step out of it
     to a hex outside every zone. A stack that fought cannot walk. Contact on
     turn 1 is therefore possible. The whole walk is one move: selecting a stack
     highlights every hex within reach (up to 2 hexes, 2 + rail with rail)
     and one click on any of them moves the stack there; a zone or rough hex is only
     ever the last step of a path.
  8. DEFENDERS AND GEAR: nobody gains gear for merely winning or holding. A
     defender who holds or repels an attack gains nothing; the attacker gains
     a gear chip only when it displaces an enemy stack, i.e. it takes the hex
     because the defender retreated or was destroyed. Other sources: rally,
     merge (gear adds) and split (gear halves). A stack can never have
     more gear than men: gain and rally stop at that cap, and gear that no
     longer fits is lost along with the men.
  9. SURRENDER: the surrender cards are gone with the jokers. Instead, at the
      end of each player's own turn the nation breaks if its public exhaustion
      meter has reached the fixed limit (superseded by item 15).
 10. TOWNS: the map has ~100 named places (big cities and fortresses, cities,
     small towns; sized by label). Each one on French or German soil belongs
     to that nation; Belgian, Luxembourg and Dutch ones are neutral and never
     change hands. A stack standing on an enemy town captures it (also by
     advancing into it after a won attack); the town is then outlined in the
     captor's colour with a flag and stays captured until a stack of the other
     side stands on it again. EXHAUSTION: at the end of each of its own turns
     a nation gains exhaustion for each town the enemy holds (see item 15 for
     the current numbers). Capitals are towns too (and still win the war when
     held a full round).
 11. SURROUNDED STACKS: a cut-off stack (item 8 of M9) now loses 1 gear chip on
     each of its owner's turns while it has gear, and 1 man each turn once the
     gear is gone, starting with the first turn.
  Simulation (40 seeded games, naive bots, thresholds raised from 40-56 to
  56-72 to make up for the exhaustion ticks): A ~29 / B ~11, ~14 rounds. Bots
  do not defend towns, and the side that moves first captures first, so the
  first-mover edge is the thing to watch in human playtests.
 12. DEFAULT SCENARIO DOCTRINES (both declared): Germany (B) has Aggressive 3
     (major), Reorganizational 2 (minor), Defensive 1, Willingness 0. France
     (A) has Willingness 3 (major), Defensive 2 (minor), Reorganizational 1,
     Aggressive 0. The tests pin their own doctrines, so they do not depend
     on this scenario. With these doctrines and the spent rule removed the
     same simulation gives A 12 / B 28 (~15 rounds): the aggressive major
     beats naive bots that do not defend. Needs human playtests before any
     tuning; candidate knobs are the surrender thresholds per nation.
 13. ARTILLERY AND EMPTY TRENCHES.
  - Shelling (reworked in item 20): during an active Offensive (spade) order,
    instead of an assault the player may pay points to shell an enemy stack
    adjacent to one of its own (not cut off). Shelling strips trench chips
    first and then takes men; the shooter loses nothing.
    Shelling counts as casualties, does not use up the order's attack and can
    be repeated while points last; unspent points are lost when the attack is
    made. It does not set the "fought" flag of any stack.
  - Empty hexes keep at most one trench chip. When a stack leaves a hex
    (walk, retreat, destruction, merge) the extra chips are removed; the
    hex's trenches are trimmed before an advancing attacker enters it.
  - Simulation (40 games): bots that shell when no winning attack is in
    reach give A 9 / B 31, ~15 rounds, casualties A 36 / B 28; with shelling
    off A 14 / B 26. Shelling mostly helps the side with the most spade points
    (Germany). Needs human playtests.
 14. FREE RECRUITS EVERY TURN.
  - At the start of each of its turns a nation may muster recruits: 1 / 1 / 2 /
    3 for Willingness doctrine level 0 / 1 / 2 / 3 (`recruits` in cards.json).
    Each recruit becomes a new 1-man stack on an empty town of the nation's
    own territory that the enemy has not taken. A recruit is not placed on a
    town where the new stack would be surrounded (cut off from home). Recruits
    are free (no card, no points) and are lost if not placed by the end of the
    turn. The new stack has used its walk. Merge (Logistics) joins them up.
  - The Home Front card's recruit (+1 man to a stack on home soil) stays as a
    surge on top; cut-off stacks cannot receive it.
  - Fix found while simulating: after a first attack the remaining points must
    still pay the extra attack's cost; shelling can spend them below that, and
    the second attack is then not offered.
  - Simulation (100 games, naive bots that muster recruits about three hexes
    behind the front): A 48 / B 52, ~14 rounds, every game ends by broken
    will, casualties A 38 / B 27. With recruits off it was A 9 / B 31 (40
    games), so the Willingness major (France) is now about on par with the
    Offensive major (Germany).
 15. PUBLIC EXHAUSTION METER (supersedes hidden thresholds, resolve, war
     weariness, battles lost, home-hex points, the declared-doctrine bonus
     and the Willingness threshold bonus of M9/M10; older notes on "break
     score", "thresholds 56-72", propaganda and secret doctrines are void).
  - Each nation has one public number: men lost + accumulated exhaustion.
    Exhaustion ticks at the end of each of the nation's own turns by a flat
    +1 (`turnTick`; this replaces any hard turn limit, every war wears down)
    plus +1 per town the enemy holds (`townWeight`, `bigTownWeight`; both 1).
  - The nation breaks, and loses, when the meter reaches the same fixed limit
    for both (`threshold`, 55), checked at the end of its own turn. There is
    no hidden information left: doctrines are always shown.
  - Home Front hearts keep recruit (2 points) and relief (1 point): relief
    lowers exhaustion by 1 (J 2, Q 3, A 3) but never below zero and never
    refunds men lost.
  - Calibration (naive bots, 40-50 games per setting): limit 45 gave games
    of ~11 rounds; 55 gives ~15 rounds. With big towns worth 2 the first
    mover took more towns and won ~75% (37/13); with both weights 1 it is
    22/18. The flat tick alone ends bot games at about 30+ rounds, so it is
    mostly a backstop. Needs human playtests.
 16. ADJACENT-STACK SUPPORT (first step toward a frontline feel).
  - A defending stack gets +1 defence for each friendly stack on an adjacent
    hex, capped at +2 (`support` in cards.json: perStack 1, max 2). It counts
    in `strength(state, stack, true)` so the board numbers, the attack preview
    and the bots all see it. It does not change the attacker's strength or the
    bloodletting (attacker losses still use terrain + trenches only), and an
    attacker never gets it. Stacks standing side by side hold better than a
    lone stack, which rewards forming a line instead of scattering.
  - Simulation (40 games each): off A 22 / B 18, ~15.6 rounds; 1/2 A 19 /
    B 21, ~17.1 rounds; 1/1 A 20 / B 20, ~16.7 rounds. Balance is unchanged
    and games run a little longer. Not done yet: flank exposure, linked
    trenches, blocking zones of control (the front line is now drawn, see 17).
 17. BIGGER HAND, IMPROVISING, NO BRACE, FRONT LINE (supersedes the hand of 5
     in item 1 and every "brace" / "Posture" rule: Defence menu, Iron wall's
     bracing, the brace upkeep of M7-M9).
  - Hand size is 7 (`handSize`). A 5-card hand had no spade 22% of the time,
    a 7-card hand 11.5%, so players are less often stuck with the wrong suit.
    The turn is unchanged: two plays and one free discard.
  - IMPROVISE: discard any two cards of the same suit (any ranks, faces
    included, their abilities are lost) to play a plain rank-2 order of any
    suit. It uses ONE play and not the free discard. Rank 2 is tier 0 and the
    doctrine shift of the chosen suit still applies. Cancelling an unused
    improvised order returns both cards and the play. The hand title and an
    order picker in the UI walk the player through it: Improvise on a card,
    pick the second card, pick the order.
  - BRACE IS GONE: stacks no longer carry a temporary defence bonus, so there
    is no per-turn upkeep. Defence points now buy only trench chips. Reserves
    (Q) gives every non-surrounded stack that touches an enemy stack one free
    trench chip (up to the order's limit) the moment it is played. Iron wall
    (K) is +1 point and one deeper trenches. Hold the line (A) is Elastic
    defence + the Reserves effect + Iron wall's bonuses. Cards whose effect
    happens on play (Elastic, Reserves) cannot be cancelled for a refund.
  - FRONT LINE: every land hex belongs to the side whose nearest stack is
    nearer (`frontOwners`); equidistant hexes belong to nobody (no man's
    land). The map draws, in each side's colour, the edges between its hexes
    and the other side's or no man's land, pulled slightly inward so the two
    lines run side by side. The header toggle "Show front line" hides it. It
    is purely a display aid: no rule depends on it.
  - Simulation (40 games): A 21 / B 19, ~17.6 rounds, casualties A 45 / B 35.
    Bots that improvise change nothing measurable (A 21 / B 19 either way).
    Needs human playtests.
18. CARD BANK: NO PLAY CAP (supersedes "two plays per turn" in items 12 and 17)
  - There is no limit on orders per turn. The hand is a bank: it starts at
    `startHand` 4, gains `drawPerTurn` 2 cards at the end of each turn, and
    never exceeds `handSize` 7 (draws beyond the cap are lost, so hoarding
    stops at 7). Saving cards builds a big offensive; a pair or an improvise
    costs two cards, i.e. a turn's income.
  - There is no free discard any more: a player may discard any number of
    cards from the hand at any time, but it does nothing (no replacement, no
    effect). It only makes room under the cap and thins the deck's turnover.
    The bots throw away cards no stack can use.
  - `cardsPerTurn` is gone; `state.played` just counts the orders played this
    turn (shown as "Orders played"). The bots play up to 8 orders a turn
    (MAX_ORDERS) and always spend what they can.
  - Simulation (40 games): A 21 / B 19, ~17.5 rounds, casualties A 44 / B 35.
    Bots never bank, so this says little about the hoarding tension.
    Needs human playtests; watch for the opening turns (4 cards each) and
    for quiet fronts that the exhaustion tick alone has to end.
19. ATTACK COST LIMIT, SUPPLY THROUGH FRIENDS
  - An attacker pays the target hex's terrain + trench chips in men (at least 1).
    It can no longer pay with its last man: a stack may only attack a hex if it
    has MORE men than that cost (`attackTargets`). So an attack can never
    destroy its own attacker, and no mutual annihilation is possible. A 1-man
    recruit stack cannot attack at all (MIN_ATTACKER_MEN 2).
  - If both nations somehow lose their last stack at once, the one with more
    casualties loses (a tie goes to the nation whose turn it is).
  - Supply: a stack is not cut off if it can trace a path home through
    adjacent friendly stacks. The hex of a friendly stack counts as passable even
    when it sits in an enemy zone, so a line of touching stacks stays supplied
    as long as one end reaches home. A group walled in on all sides is still cut off.
  - Sim (40 games): A 21 / B 19, ~18 rounds, casualties A 44 / B 35.
  - Supply range: the path must also be at most `supplyRange` (8, was 10) hexes long and
    end at a SUPPLY DEPOT: the capital, or a town the nation holds (its own home
    town the enemy has not taken, or an enemy town it has captured). The depot
    must lie outside enemy zones (the capital is exempt). A stack that has pushed
    too far past its last town is cut off and bleeds like a surrounded one.
    Trial (30 games): range 6 A 9 / B 21, 8: 13 / 17, 10: 14 / 16, unlimited:
    17 / 13 - the shorter the range, the harder it is for the aggressor.
  - A cut-off stack exerts no zone of control (`inEnemyZoc` ignores it). Supply
    paths themselves are judged against the full zones (`inRawZoc`) so the two
    rules cannot loop.
20. SETS, BOMBARDMENT, RAIL, ROUGH-GROUND STOP
  - Sets: see item 4. No cap on the set size (the hand holds 7); the sum is
    linear, so merging cards only concentrates points in one order and gives
    up the separate free attacks and face abilities. Low spade pairs are worse
    than playing the cards alone (3+3 = 0+0+1 points), high pairs better
    (8+8 = 3+3+1 = 7, was 5).
  - Bombardment: each point strips 1 trench chip from the target's hex (2 with
    Surprise), but never below 1 chip (SHELL_TRENCH_FLOOR). When the hex is
    down to 1 chip (or had none) a shell takes 1 man and costs `extraCost`
    points (2, or 1 with Combined arms), never below 2 men (SHELL_FLOOR). A
    stack at 2 men on a hex with at most 1 chip cannot be shelled. Attackers
    pay the hex's chips in men, so stripping chips also makes the follow-up
    attack cheaper (and the "men must exceed the cost" limit easier).
  - Rail: once per stack per turn (a stack that already has rail is not
    eligible), +2 hexes (+3 with Rail net/Mobilisation), and it ignores the
    rough-ground stop.
  - Rough-ground stop: a walk ends on a hex with terrain or trench chips
    (`fortification` > 0), like an enemy zone (stack.stalled); about half the
    land hexes are clear, and river hexes always stop a stack.
  - `supplyRange` 8.
  - Simulation (100 games, naive bots that never rail): A 22 / B 78, ~20.5
    rounds, casualties A 51 / B 24. Splits: shelling off, A 23 / B 37; pairs
    and shelling off, A 22 / B 38; pairs off only, A 16 / B 44; supply range 10
    instead of 8, A 17 / B 43. The shelling skew (the Offensive major gains
    most) was already there with the old rules (A 9 / B 31); the bots pair only
    spades. Needs human playtests; knobs: man cost, trench floor, supplyRange.
